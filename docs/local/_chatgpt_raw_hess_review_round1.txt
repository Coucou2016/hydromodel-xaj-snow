HESS-style referee report

Overall recommendation: reconsider after major extension / reject-and-resubmit in its present pilot form.

The two-basin experiment is internally useful and, importantly, the authors are already avoiding the weakest novelty claim (“first XAJ snow model”). Basin A provides a strong proof-of-concept signal: adding the minimal snow routine changes test NSE from −0.232 to 0.732 and KGE from 0.210 to 0.776, whereas the snow-free Basin B remains essentially unchanged. That is a good reason to proceed with the study.

However, it is not yet enough for the paper that the Introduction currently wants to become. The central scientific contribution is explicitly multi-regional failure diagnosis, selective structural repair, and an applicability boundary. Those are exactly the analyses that remain pending. With only one snow basin and one negative control, the manuscript currently demonstrates a compelling case study, not a population-level diagnosis or an applicability boundary.

The novelty bar is also higher than the draft currently assumes. Most importantly, Chen and Zhao (2025) have already coupled CemaNeige directly to XAJ and evaluated variants across 531 CAMELS catchments, reporting an increase in median KGE from 0.58 to 0.68 attributable to adding CemaNeige, with further gains from differentiable parameter learning. Thus, “multi-basin XAJ + CemaNeige” is no longer a defensible novelty claim by itself.

Major Concern 3 — rep=800 does not yet establish a fair comparison between the 15- and 17-parameter models

I agree with the authors' own caution here. “Both models received 800 SCE-UA repetitions/evaluations” is not by itself a fairness argument.

There are two distinct issues:

Optimization fairness. A 17-dimensional search can require a different effective budget from a 15-dimensional search. Depending on the specific SCE-UA implementation, dimensionality may also influence population construction and hence how much of a fixed budget is spent before convergence. Therefore equal nominal rep is not necessarily equal convergence.

Structural-flexibility fairness. Even with complete convergence, XAJ-Snow has two additional calibrated degrees of freedom. A snow-free negative control helps test whether those extra parameters indiscriminately improve streamflow, but one control basin cannot rule this out generally.

I would require a compact optimizer × parameter-freedom experiment. At minimum:

Experiment	XAJ parameters	Snow parameters	Purpose
MZ	optimize 15	absent	baseline
Snow-freeze-XAJ	freeze at MZ optimum	optimize Kf, CTG	isolates snow intervention
Snow-fixed	optimize 15	fixed/predefined Kf, CTG	tests structural addition without two locally optimized freedoms
Snow-joint	optimize 15	optimize Kf, CTG	full proposed model

The particularly valuable arm is freeze the non-snow parameters and calibrate only the snow coefficient(s). Premier et al. (2026) provides a relevant methodological precedent for testing snow-process parameters while holding the rest of a hydrological model calibration fixed. 
HESS

For optimization robustness, repeat at increasing budgets—for example 800, 1600 and 3200 or until an empirical plateau is visible—and use multiple independent seeds. The exact values can differ; what matters is showing that the sign and magnitude of ΔKGE are not a consequence of premature convergence.

I would not demand that every production basin receive an enormous budget if a pilot convergence analysis demonstrates that 800 is adequate. But that evidence needs to exist.

Major Concern 4 — The improvement is consistent with a snow-storage deficiency, but does not yet identify that mechanism

The current Discussion comes too close to causal attribution.

The contrast

It does not prove it.

process structure;

meteorological input use;

temporal precipitation-to-runoff transformation;

two calibrated parameters.

Moreover, the manuscript uses Caravan forcing, and Clerc-Schwarzenbach et al. showed that differences between Caravan and original CAMELS meteorological forcing can substantially affect model calibration, with precipitation differences responsible for much of the performance degradation and large differences also occurring in PET. 
HESS

5. Results and Discussion: main logic breaks
Logic break A: “similar HFT + snow difference ⇒ snow causes the performance difference”

No. Similar HFT removes one possible gross contrast. It does not make the basins a matched causal pair.

“The contrast is consistent with the snow-omission hypothesis and motivates a population-level test; it does not uniquely attribute the difference to snow.”

Logic break B: “Basin B is flat ⇒ 17-vs-15 flexibility concern is resolved”

Too strong.

Basin B demonstrates that the extra snow parameters do not automatically improve this basin. That is useful and should remain.

Logic break C: “interior Kf and CTG ⇒ physically reasonable process”

Interior calibration tells you the optimizer is not simply hitting your bounds. That is different from physical identifiability.

Logic break D: hydrograph improvement ⇒ correct SWE

The spring hydrograph evidence shows that including the T-dependent snow routine greatly improves temporal runoff behaviour. It does not establish that accumulated SWE or melt flux is correct.

“The pilot establishes feasibility and justifies the planned large-sample experiment, but it does not establish a general snow-related applicability boundary for XAJ-MZ. Such a claim requires population-level selectivity, optimization/freedom robustness, and snow-state consistency analyses.”

After those analyses are complete, the final conclusion should answer three quantitative questions:

Where does baseline XAJ skill deteriorate?

Where does the minimal snow intervention recover skill?

How uncertain is the inferred transition?

That is the paper.
