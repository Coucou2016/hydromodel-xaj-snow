## 1 Intro logic

The revised Introduction now has a defensible logical spine: structural inadequacy in regime-specific settings → existing XAJ–snow prior art → narrower unresolved diagnostic question → controlled intervention design → RQs → explicit statement of current evidence status. That is substantially better aligned with the novelty boundary from Round 1. The sequence at L48–57 works particularly well: prior art is conceded before the gap is asserted, rather than presenting XAJ-Snow itself as the contribution.

The main problem is repetition after the gap has already been established. L55 ("This manuscript does not claim…"), L58 ("Rather than proposing…"), and L63–65 ("Relative to Chen… / Wu… / Wang…") all make essentially the same distinction: the snow module is established; the intended contribution is the controlled diagnostic protocol. I would compress L58–65 to one paragraph. A cleaner structure is:
- L48–55: problem + prior art + explicit novelty boundary.
- L56–57: unresolved question and why it requires controls.
- L58–60: study objective/design.
- One sentence, not three, positioning against Chen/Wu/large-sample diagnostics.
- RQs.

There is also one substantive jump at L50. The sentence states that models lacking snow storage "mis-time spring peaks," but the current main experiment does not define a quantitative peak-timing or seasonal-timing diagnostic; later, Fig. 2 is interpreted qualitatively as showing systematic peak misplacement. Either soften the Introduction to "can misrepresent seasonal storage and runoff timing" or eventually add an explicit timing diagnostic. Otherwise the Introduction promises a process-specific symptom that the quantitative Results do not formally test.

L63 is still too strong in one place: the sentence asks when the snow layer is "necessary" for structural adequacy and contrasts that with a "high-capacity XAJ–snow learner." Even the completed 80-basin study could establish that adding the snow process is associated with selective and robust skill recovery; it would not prove logical necessity of that particular formulation. I would use "when adding an explicit snow store yields improvements consistent with correction of an omitted-process limitation." The "high-capacity" characterization of Chen is also unnecessary for the argument.

The RQs are conceptually aligned with the gap, but two need stronger methodological closure. RQ2 asks whether XAJ-Snow remains "neutral" on negative controls, yet Methods never defines an operational neutrality criterion. RQ3 combines two distinct inferential problems—optimization/parameter-freedom robustness and estimation of an applicability relationship. Define neutrality prospectively, without inventing the threshold after seeing results, and either split RQ3 or give its two components separate Methods subsections.

Finally, L73–74 are useful in this working draft because they prevent readers from mistaking the 14-basin screen for the final study. They should disappear from a submission-ready Introduction once the planned analyses are complete; "the present draft reports…" and "full answers remain incomplete" are project-status statements rather than manuscript argument.

## 2 Methods logic

At the highest level, the order is correct: data → baseline model → snow-enabled model → calibration/metrics → negative-control design. The major reproducibility problem is not ordering but that Section 2.1 describes only the two pilot basins, whereas Section 3.4 suddenly introduces a 14-basin experiment whose sampling protocol and strata have not been defined.

This is the most important Methods revision. Before the model subsections, add a short study design / basin sampling subsection defining: the source and calculation of frac_snow; how the 14 basins were selected; the basin IDs or a supplementary table; whether frac_snow ≥ 0.1 and >0.3 were prespecified or exploratory descriptive bins; and how the future frozen sample was stratified. The public paired table verifies the 14 observations and their snow-bin labels, but the accompanying diagnostic note points to results/sampling/sample_batch1.csv, which is not present at the commit-pinned raw path I checked. That is a genuine reproducibility gap even if the numerical paired results themselves are available.

Relatedly, the S2 category appears for the first time in Results: L202 defines it there as frac_snow > 0.3. That is too late. All grouping rules used to summarize performance should be introduced in Methods. If the batch was genuinely exploratory and the bins were selected after looking at the data, say so explicitly rather than retrospectively presenting them as a formal design.

The Gthreshold disclosure is in the right location: immediately after the snow equations in Section 2.3. The code confirms the manuscript description: when no threshold is supplied, the snow routine calculates it as 0.9 times mean annual snowfall from the precipitation/temperature series supplied to that particular call. The issue is not placement but inferential interpretation. Because training and testing are loaded separately, the test simulation uses a climatological quantity computed from the complete test-period forcing. That is not calibration against test discharge and is not an additional fitted parameter, but it does make the test simulation depend on a full-period test-forcing statistic. For a strict out-of-sample argument, I would either make a training-derived/frozen Gthreshold the primary protocol or, at minimum, add it as an explicit sensitivity check. The current L114 sentence is transparent, which is good, but "not a hidden degree of freedom" does not resolve the train/test-independence question.

Several other reproducibility details should move into Methods before the final paper:
- rep and ngs need plain-language definitions, not merely software names. State what rep=800 actually limits, what ngs=15 represents, the stopping rules, seeds, and whether repeated runs use identical seeds across models. L119 currently calls this an "identical budget by configuration," which is accurate only at the configuration level.
- List or reference all 15 XAJ-MZ calibrated parameters and their search ranges; the snow implementation clearly has 17 total parameters, but the manuscript only defines the two additions. The code confirms the 15+2 structure and exact parameter ordering.
- State snow-state initialization and warm-up handling explicitly. The implementation exposes a 365-day warm-up and the snow routine has initial SWE/thermal-state defaults, but the manuscript does not explain whether snow states are zero-initialized before warm-up or transferred across periods.
- L121 is structurally misplaced because it contains a completed result inside Methods: the rep=2000 NSE values. Methods should state that a partial higher-budget sensitivity was conducted; the numerical outcome belongs in Results. The repository independently confirms XAJ-MZ -0.3106 and XAJ-Snow 0.7318 at rep=2000, while the control and rep=5000 cases remain incomplete.
- L136, beginning "Human-footprint attributes…", is also not method. It interprets the pilot pair as having "falsified" a human-disturbance explanation. Move it to Discussion and soften it; similar footprint values do not causally exclude anthropogenic effects.

One smaller technical issue: L132 says KGE values near zero indicate severe degradation. Avoid giving KGE an NSE-like zero interpretation. KGE does not have the same inherent mean-flow benchmark at zero that NSE does; this distinction is explicitly discussed by Knoben et al. (HESS). Define the exact KGE formulation and state simply that 1 is optimal and lower values indicate increasing departure in its constituent components.

## 3 Results logic

The present sequence—pilot table → SciPy refine → five pilot figures → batch1 → pending results—does not optimally support the Discussion. I would reorder the completed evidence as: 3.1 Paired pilot performance and negative control → 3.2 Pilot hydrograph diagnostics → 3.3 First-look multi-basin screening → 3.4 Partial optimizer-budget sensitivity.

The SciPy refine should move to the Supplement. It changes both the search stage and the optimization objective from the primary SCE-UA+KGE experiment, and it exists only for 01013500. The manuscript correctly labels it a supplementary illustration rather than a fairness proof, but placing the 0.8779 versus 0.1393 contrast immediately after the primary matched experiment gives it disproportionate narrative weight. In the main text, one sentence can refer readers to the Supplement as an objective/search sensitivity.

Similarly, five figures devoted to the two-basin pilot before reaching the 14-basin evidence overweights the engineering pilot. Figures 1–4 have distinct roles, but Fig. 5 is comparatively redundant for the central structural argument. For a final HESS paper, I would retain only the most diagnostic pilot visualization(s) in the main paper and move supplementary pilot views out once the multi-basin results are complete.

The completed rep=2000 result should be promoted from Methods to Results. It is useful precisely because it is bounded: on 01013500, increasing the available SCE-UA budget to rep=2000 does not reverse the pilot contrast, but because only one basin/model pair sensitivity is complete and there is no corresponding higher-budget negative control or multi-seed factorial, it cannot establish budget robustness. That is a legitimate sensitivity result when stated at that level.

L203 needs revision even though its numbers are publicly verifiable. The sentence beginning "The all-sample median…" reports 0.0088 and then explains why "manuscript emphasis therefore stays on stratified summaries." The public paired data also show very poor performance for both models in some basins, including extreme cases. The logical problem is the direction of justification: stratification should be justified by the prespecified scientific hypothesis about snow exposure, not because the unstratified median is unhelpful. Otherwise it reads as outcome-driven emphasis. Report the all-sample result, then state whether snow stratification was prespecified; if not, label it exploratory.

The sentence interpreting Fig. 2 as XAJ-MZ "systematically" misplacing snow-season peaks is also stronger than the completed quantitative evidence. With no formal peak-timing metric among the locked completed facts, describe this as a qualitative hydrograph pattern rather than a demonstrated systematic timing error.

The "Pending Results" block at L220–230 should not remain in Results. In the current internal working draft, place these items under a Methods subsection such as "Planned full-sample analyses" or an internal status note. In the submission manuscript, remove the block entirely. If applicability, SWE, and fairness remain necessary to answer the stated RQs, the correct solution is to complete them or narrow the RQs—not to move unfinished results into Conclusions.

## 4 Discussion audit

Paragraph beginning L233 — REVISE. The sentence beginning "The pilot is consistent…" risks circular attribution. The completed evidence is that XAJ-Snow greatly improves one snow-affected basin and is nearly neutral in one low-snow basin. That is consistent with the snow-deficiency hypothesis, but because the intervention also changes parameter dimensionality and there is no completed SWE consistency test, it does not identify the causal deficiency as snow storage/melt. Under your strict completed-facts rule, the human-footprint argument also lies outside the performance evidence you authorized. I would frame this as evidence consistent with, but not diagnostic of, an omitted snow-process limitation.

L234 — ADOPT. This is one of the strongest Discussion sentences. It correctly says that the near-zero control response reduces, but does not eliminate, concern about the extra two parameters, and it explicitly requires a larger zero-snow cohort/fixed-parameter ablation. That inference follows from the completed two-basin pilot.

L235–239 — substantially compress. These sentences mostly repeat the Introduction's novelty positioning instead of interpreting Results. One concise literature-comparison paragraph is enough. Two specific wording risks remain: L236's "structurally required" is stronger than the available inference, and L239 ("Our contribution is complementary…") lists pending applicability/fairness diagnostics as though they are already delivered. Until those analyses exist, call them the intended study design or planned diagnostic tests, not completed contributions.

L238 itself is not circular, because it is literature synthesis rather than interpretation of your data. The problem is placement and duplication: the same comparison to Chen/Wu/Wang/Liu has already been made in detail in the Introduction. Discussion space would be better spent asking what the observed pilot and first-look patterns do and do not support.

L240 — acceptable. It explicitly describes future work and does not pretend the fixed-parameter isolation experiment is complete.

L241–242 — ADOPT. These are appropriately conservative. They explicitly retain forcing, PET, spatial temperature representation, equifinality, anthropogenic effects, and missing snow-state constraints as alternative explanations/limitations. This is the right epistemic posture for the current evidence.

L243 — revise. The statement that interior Kf/CTG values "support numerical stability" is not demonstrated by one selected calibration. Interior values show only that the selected optimum is not pinned to the parameter bounds. Numerical/search stability would require repeated seeds, convergence behavior, or comparable evidence—the very tests that remain pending.

Two completed evidence blocks are conspicuously missing from Discussion.

First, the 14-basin first-look screen should have its own paragraph. The allowed facts—median ΔNSE +0.546 for frac_snow ≥ 0.1 (n=9), -0.007 for <0.1 (n=5), and S2 +0.584 (n=5)—are sufficient to say that the first-look sample reproduces the directional specificity motivating the larger experiment, but not sufficient to infer an applicability threshold or population relationship. The repository values agree with those summaries. Critically, remind the reader here that these runs used rep=200, not the pilot's rep=800.

Second, discuss the partial rep=2000 sensitivity. XAJ-Snow remains at 0.7318 NSE on 01013500 while XAJ-MZ is -0.3106; this means the contrast was not reversed in that one completed higher-budget case. It does not demonstrate optimizer robustness because there is no completed control-basin rep=2000, rep=5000, or multi-seed factorial.

So the circular reasoning to eliminate is: snow basin improves after adding snow → therefore original failure was caused by missing snow physics.

At present, the defensible chain is narrower: one snow-affected basin shows large paired recovery + one low-snow basin shows near-neutral response + a small reduced-budget batch shows snow-stratified directional differences → sufficient evidence to continue the controlled structural-diagnosis experiment, not yet sufficient to identify a general causal or applicability boundary.

## 5 batch1 framing

I recommend replacing the current heading "First-look multi-basin extension" with wording that makes both exploratory status and the reduced calibration budget visible before the numbers are read.

Proposed heading: "3.3 First-look multi-basin screening under a reduced calibration budget"

Proposed lead paragraph: "To assess whether the two-basin pilot warranted completion of the planned stratified experiment, we conducted an exploratory paired screening of 14 CAMELS basins using the same training and test periods and the same SCE-UA–KGE objective as the pilot, but with a lighter calibration budget (rep = 200 rather than rep = 800). These runs are therefore treated as screening evidence rather than as budget-equivalent replication or population-level inference. Within this first-look sample, the median test-period ΔNSE (XAJ-Snow − XAJ-MZ) was +0.5461 for basins with frac_snow ≥ 0.1 (n = 9) and −0.0068 for basins with frac_snow < 0.1 (n = 5); for the S2 subset (frac_snow > 0.3, n = 5), the median ΔNSE was +0.5835. These descriptive contrasts motivate completion of the frozen stratified sample and calibration-fairness analyses, but they do not establish an applicability threshold or population-level snow-response relationship."

I would also remove the current follow-on sentence saying that manuscript "emphasis therefore stays on stratified summaries." Instead, establish the snow stratification in Methods and then report both the overall and stratified summaries without allowing the observed all-sample median to determine which result receives emphasis.

## 6 Abstract consistency

I found no numerical contradiction between the Abstract and the completed primary results in the body. The pilot NSE/KGE values, control response, Kf/CTG values, SciPy refine values, and 14-basin stratified medians all match the corresponding Results and/or public diagnostic files.

There are, however, several claim-architecture issues.

The Abstract's L37 phrase about testing under "matched budgets" is technically consistent with equal rep configurations, but could be read as saying the fairness problem is already solved. The body explicitly says it is not: the 17-versus-15 parameter issue, multi-seed tests, fixed-snow-parameter ablations, and larger budgets remain incomplete. I would write "matched optimizer configurations" or "matched nominal calibration budgets" until the fairness factorial is complete.

The Abstract's SciPy refine sentence is numerically substantiated, but if the refine moves to the Supplement—as I recommend—it should also leave the Abstract. Otherwise a secondary, differently optimized one-basin result receives equal Abstract status with the primary matched comparison.

Likewise, L42's interior Kf/CTG values are supported, but they will probably not deserve Abstract space once the large-sample results exist. More importantly, do not let "interior" imply physical identification or optimization stability; the Discussion itself correctly recognizes the absence of SWE constraints.

L45's explicit "Pending" inventory is fully consistent with the body, but it is appropriate only for this working draft. A final HESS Abstract cannot contain a list of analyses still to be performed. Once experiments are complete, replace it with actual evidence; if some tests remain outside scope, narrow the claims instead.

Conversely, three body claims need attention even though their absence from the Abstract is not itself a problem. L87/L136's human-activity interpretation is too strong; L176's systematic snow-season timing statement lacks a completed quantitative timing diagnostic under the evidence set you specified; and L243's "numerical stability" inference exceeds what interior parameter values establish. These should be softened in the body rather than promoted into the Abstract.
